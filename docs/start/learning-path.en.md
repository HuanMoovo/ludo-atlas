# Ludo Atlas · Getting Started · Learning Paths

> **Getting Started**. Three routes: **hobby, indie, job hunting**. Different goals, different commitments, different things to practice. Choose a route first, then advance stage by stage.

## 1. The Three Routes at a Glance

| Route | Goal | Suggested time commitment | Timeline | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hobby | Enjoy yourself; make small works | 3-6 hours a week | Open-ended | Fun first, free choice of projects |
| Indie | Make a game that sells | 15-40 hours a week | Starting at 1-3 years | Completion, publishing, survival |
| Job hunting | Get a job in the industry | 2-4 hours a day + intensive periods | 6-18 months | Role fit, portfolio, interviews |

## 2. General Principles

1. **Learn by doing**: projects drive everything; tutorials are only reference books. Every stage must produce something that runs and that you can show to people.
2. **Escalating projects**: start with a project you can finish in 2 weeks; after each one, move the difficulty up a level (see the project list in [Your First Game](first-game.md)).
3. **Time-box getting stuck**: if one problem blocks you for more than 2 hours, change paths (search, ask, work around it); don't grind it out.
4. **Join feedback loops**: game dev events, communities, friends playtesting. Learning without feedback stalls at feeling good about yourself.
5. **Make progress public**: publish your devlogs; it is both output training and a source of opportunities.

## 3. Route 1: Hobby

| Stage | What to do | Reference | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0-1 month | Pick an engine, finish the official getting-started tutorial, make your first small game | [Engine Selection Guide](engine-choice.md) · [Your First Game](first-game.md) | Publish to itch.io (it can be just for friends) |
| 1-6 months | One small project per month; try 2-3 different genres | [Genre Handbooks](../genres/README.md) | 3-5 playable works accumulated |
| 6 months+ | Pick a project you like and make it presentable | [Game Design Handbook](../fundamentals/game-design/README.md) | 1 work you are willing to show to strangers |

## 4. Route 2: Indie

| Stage | What to do | Reference | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation (1-3 months) | Finish 2-3 small projects; identify the limits of your ability and your style preferences | [Your First Game](first-game.md) | Can finish a small game independently and publish it |
| Skill building (3-12 months) | Fill key gaps for your chosen genre (level design / balance / audio / publishing) | [Genre Handbooks](../genres/README.md) · [Art & Audio Handbook](../fundamentals/art-audio/README.md) | 1-2 complete works, including a store page and marketing materials |
| First commercial title (1-3 years) | One-page kickoff → prototype → vertical slice → release | [Production Handbook](../fundamentals/production/README.md) · [Multi-platform Launch Playbook](../../playbooks/platform-launch/README.md) | Launched, with data collected (sales, wishlists, reviews) |
| Long term | Postmortems, iteration, cash-flow control | [Indie Survival Playbook](../../playbooks/indie-survival/README.md) | Can keep developing a second and third title |

Risk warning: the indie route has a high failure rate; survival strategy (savings, part-time work, scope control) matters as much as development skill.

## 5. Route 3: Job Hunting

| Stage | What to do | Reference | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Targeting (1 month) | Decide on target roles (see [Roles & Skills Map](role-map.md)); collect 20 job descriptions (JDs) for those roles | [Roles & Skills Map](role-map.md) | A distilled list of the most frequently required skills |
| Skills (3-9 months) | Targeted training against the list; keep fixed daily time for code / art / design | The matching handbooks (Game Design Handbook, Programming Handbook, Art & Audio Handbook, etc.) | 2-3 role-relevant works completed |
| Portfolio (1-2 months) | Polish 3 works: one complete, one flashy, one role-matched | [Open Source Picks & Book Recommendations](../../resources/books-and-repos.md) | A portfolio page you can share |
| Applications & interviews | Resume, test tasks, behavioral interviews, industry contacts | To be added | An offer, or clear feedback on the gaps |

Common interview topics: for engineering roles, data structures and algorithms, engine mechanics and principles, and detailed postmortems of your projects; for design roles, case breakdowns and test-task work; for art roles, portfolio + test task. One bonus point shared by all roles: a project you finished and released on your own initiative.

## 6. Self-Check Checkpoints per Stage

- Month 1: can I build a small feature on my own without copying a tutorial?
- Month 3: has anyone (even a friend) played my work for more than 10 minutes?
- Month 6: can I explain clearly why a game I have played is fun, and how it was made?
- Month 12: do I have one public record of a year of continuous work (a log, a body of work, a skill tree)?

## 7. Using This Repository Alongside the Routes

1. Read the five Getting Started pages (this section) through once, then settle on a route.
2. While working, use the matching handbooks as reference books; do not read them cover to cover.
3. Write a postmortem after each finished project, and revisit [Pitfalls & Anti-patterns](../pitfalls/README.md) to check against the pitfalls you hit.
4. Re-read this page once a quarter to recalibrate your direction.
