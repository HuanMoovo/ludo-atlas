# Ludo Atlas · Indie Developers & Companies

> Positioning: meet "the people and companies behind these games": indie developers (international and Chinese), industry legends, and company rosters (platform holders / Japanese companies / Western majors / indie publishers / legendary studios / Chinese companies), plus methods for "learning from people".
> Selection criteria: public influence and representativeness; one line per person, with no ranking and no endorsement implied; facts follow public sources.
> Companions: Game History (the era each work belongs to) · Open Source Picks & Book Recommendations · Resources (entry points for interviews and talks, §3–§4).

## 6. How to "Learn from People" (Action Checklist)

1. **Read primary material**: interviews, GDC talks and devlogs come before secondhand summaries (entry points in Resources §3–§4).
2. **Play the signature works**: pick one game per developer, play it for an hour, and play with a question in mind: "Why did they do it this way?"
3. **Find the "style signature"**: compare different works by the same author (auteur-style breakdown), looking at art signatures, system preferences and narrative motifs.
4. **Follow the pulse**: follow their social media, blogs and Discord; what they are wrestling with right now is more useful than their résumé.
5. **Build your own lineage map**: connect the works you love by lines of who influenced whom, and clear design schools will emerge.
6. **Don't forget the other side of companies**: announcements, financial reports, lawsuits and failure cases carry as much information as the works themselves (see Legal, Patents & Competition and Game History).
